# One-Liner Laughs — candidates

Judge criteria (in order): (1) a real one-line joke with setup + payoff,
(2) laugh-out-loud funny, (3) surprise twist, (4) fresh/original angle,
(5) tight, every word earns its place. Max 200 characters, single line.

## Candidates

1. I asked AI to write my wedding vows; it said, "I do — as an AI language model."
2. My smart fridge judges my midnight snacks, so I unplugged it — now it just gives me the cold shoulder.
3. I told my GPS I wanted the scenic route; it drove past my ex's house and said, "Recalculating your life choices."
4. My bank account and my gym membership have the same problem: I only visit either one when I feel guilty.
5. I named my dog Five Miles so I can honestly tell people I walk Five Miles every day.
6. My doctor told me to watch my drinking, so now I drink in front of a mirror — accountability, but make it fun.
7. I asked the librarian for books on paranoia. She whispered, "They're right behind you."
8. My alarm clock and I have an agreement: it rings, I ignore it, and we both pretend I'm a morning person.
9. I tried to start a band called 1023MB — we never got a gig.
10. My plants died because I watered them with good intentions and tap water.

## Judging (as the judge)

- #1: clean setup/payoff, the twist lands on a phrase everyone recognises
  ("as an AI language model"), fresh and topical, 80 chars, zero waste.
  The surprise is the payoff itself — the vows are hijacked by the disclaimer.
- #2: good pun ("cold shoulder") but the twist is telegraphed once "smart fridge
  judges" appears; the reader sees the pun coming.
- #3: funny image, but long and the payoff is a second joke stacked on the first.
- #4: relatable, but a smile, not a laugh — no real twist, just a comparison.
- #5: known joke structure (dog named after a distance); not fresh.
- #6: "watch my drinking" wordplay is a classic; the mirror bit is decent but the
  line explains itself too much.
- #7: a well-known joke; fails freshness outright.
- #8: gentle observation, no surprise — fails criterion 3.
- #9: a pun for programmers only; the audience has to decode "1023MB" first.
- #10: "good intentions" is a nice turn, but the payoff is soft and the line
  meanders.

## Pick

**#1** — strongest on every criterion: a real one-liner, an audible laugh from
the recognisable disclaimer twist, fresh (no one has married the disclaimer
before), and 80 characters with nothing to cut.
